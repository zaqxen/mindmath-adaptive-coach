    Targets and research

    These are training targets, not population percentiles or age, occupation or IQ assessments. Digit count alone does not predict difficulty. Carrying, tables, strategy, presentation and typing affect time. Multiplication uses a 1–4 digit number × a single digit; division reverses those products. Addition and subtraction use two numbers of the selected length.

    Full-score targets in seconds for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 digits: addition 2 / 4 / 7 / 10; subtraction 2 / 5 / 8 / 12; multiplication 2 / 6 / 10 / 15; division 2 / 6 / 11 / 17. These app-defined, aspirational targets support personal comparison; they are not taken from grade or profession norms. The two-second scoring floor gives no bonus for sub-two-second responses. Percentages use 10%, 25% or 50% of 1–4 digit multiples of four, with targets 2 / 5 / 8 / 12 seconds. No comparable adult norms were established for this test.

    XtraMath uses a six-second basic-fact proficiency target. Arithmetic research distinguishes calculation, decision and response stages. Laboratory verification times cannot be treated as norms for typed answers here.

    Ranks use school and career themes as game titles. They do not establish equivalence to actual children, teachers, accountants or mathematicians. Two samples per digit band give a provisional estimate. Repeat assessments to confirm progress.
